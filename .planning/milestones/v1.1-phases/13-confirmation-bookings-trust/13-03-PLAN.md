<objective>
Build the two facts every payment-state surface in this phase needs: which rail a payment used, and
how long a refund on that rail takes to reappear — and pin the two invariants a plan could otherwise
erode.

Purpose: 13-RESEARCH's closing recommendation is *"treat the reversed state as the phase's highest-risk
surface and plan it first — it is the only one where a locked decision collides with a data gap."*
`booking.payment_method` is NULL on every reversed row by construction, so the rail must be recovered
by a live probe (D-84). 13-PATTERNS § No Analog Found records that `paymongoFetch` sets **no
`AbortSignal` anywhere in `src/`** and `src/lib/payments/config.ts:101-109` already carries that as a
known open gap — so the timeout is net-new infrastructure on a money-path file, which is why it gets
its own plan and its own acceptance criteria. D-83 narrows the permitted refund copy to three verified
numbers; giving them one owner is what makes a fourth impossible rather than merely discouraged.

Output: a widened `getCheckoutSession` whose default behaviour is unchanged, a never-throwing
booker-facing probe wrapper, the refund-window module, and a design assertion pinning `qrph`'s
exclusion and the zero-migration count.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| app server → PayMongo REST | An outbound third-party call on a money surface, on the render path of a page that exists to explain a payment failure |
| provider error prose → app surfaces | PayMongo's error text must never reach a booker response, a log line a booker can trigger, or an audit row |
| copy constant → booker's bank statement | A refund window the provider does not honour is a promise the app cannot keep |
