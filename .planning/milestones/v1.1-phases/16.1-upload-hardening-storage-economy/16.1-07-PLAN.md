Purpose: `16.1-VALIDATION.md` states the limit once so no plan has to re-argue it — **CI can prove
every decision our code makes** (the equality gate, the destroy ordering, the shared arrays, the
refusal matcher) and **CI can prove nothing about what Cloudinary actually stores**, because the test
jobs hold no Cloudinary credential and that is a deliberate project invariant recorded at
`.github/workflows/ci.yml:151` and `:875` (`T-11-CISECRET`). The six credential-bearing checks are not
a gap to be closed. **They are the phase's real gate.** This plan makes them followable and runs them.

It also does the one thing criterion 4 asks for that is not code: **record the backfill decision** —
and re-confirm the fact it rests on rather than inheriting a line.

Output: `16.1-UAT.md`, a preset that exists on the account, and six recorded results.

**This plan is not autonomous, and the reason is structural rather than cautious.** Every step below
needs either a Cloudinary credential (which CI does not hold and will not be given) or a human with a
phone, a browser and an eye. An executor cannot satisfy these as commands, so they are written as
steps a person follows with a result to paste — not as acceptance criteria dressed up to look
automated.

**One decision recorded here because this is where it becomes visible.** RESEARCH Open Question 1:
the widget renders its own English error inside its cross-origin iframe **and** fires `onError`, so
the host sees Cloudinary's sentence AND our toast. The recommendation, taken: **ship beside it and
observe.** D-179 says the phase's visible surface is error copy, not widget skinning, and the widget's
`text` option is undocumented for the error-callback path. If the doubled message reads badly in the
walk, that is a finding for the PM, not a licence to spend the phase fighting vendor chrome.
