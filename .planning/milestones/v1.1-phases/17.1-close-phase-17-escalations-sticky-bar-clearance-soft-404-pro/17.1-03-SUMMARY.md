## The finding — the band hides the sentence that makes the escape hatch safe

No interactive control is occluded across 640–1023px. The last one clears the bar by **4px** in court
and **5px** in grove. At the 320px floor the same control clears by **36px** and **37px** — a ninefold
collapse whose entire cause is the 32px `sm:py-12` takes back from the unvariant clearance.

But a leaf paragraph **is** behind the bar, at all five band viewports where the document scrolls:

| | box (court · 640×800) |
|---|---|
| `p("We'll keep your hold — the timer keeps r")` | `{x: 16, y: 736, width: 608, height: 16, bottom: 752}` |
| `[data-testid="checkout-sticky-bar"]` | `{x: 0, y: 736, width: 640, height: 64, bottom: 800}` |

**15.6px of a 16px line is behind the bar** (grove: 16.45 of 17.33). That paragraph is `HOLD_PROMISE`
in `src/components/booking/way-back-link.tsx:78` — *"We'll keep your hold — the timer keeps running."*
— and that component's own header calls it the thing that makes the link above it safe to press:

> *"a booker who does not know it survives a look at the listing will sit on this page rather than
> check the thing they wanted to check. The sentence is rendered by this component rather than left to
> the call site, so the control and its promise cannot be separated by an edit to either one."*

Across 640–1023px they **are** separated — by the confirm bar. `shell.spec.ts:1221` pins exactly one
anchor in `<main>` on this route, and this is it: the checkout's single escape hatch, with its
reassurance painted over, on the one page where the alternative to reading is paying.

That is why outcome B is *"a weaker but real defect"* rather than a shrug, and it is why the watched
red had to come from the computed-style clause: the occlusion clause is **green** there and will stay
green until one extra line of copy spends the remaining four pixels.

## Decisions Made

- **768×1024 is excluded from the permanent case, and the exclusion is a measurement.** § P2 read
  `maxScroll 0` there — the checkout document is *shorter than that viewport* (`doc 1024`, `<main>`
  ending at 841). `expectBarDoesNotOcclude`'s third vacuity guard exists to refuse exactly that
  reading, so a case pinned at that height would red on the guard rather than on the geometry: a worse
  assertion wearing a better-looking number. The three heights driven (568 / 600 / 600) all scroll.
- **The padding is compared against the rendered bar height, and the end-of-document element is a
  parameter.** Both halves matter. `4636206` repaired the sibling route by moving the clearance onto
  the layout wrapper, which *lowers* that `<main>`'s padding while making the document more correct —
  a clause pinned to the declared spelling would red on the fix. A Shape-B repair on checkout moves
  the **selector**, in the same commit, which is a smaller and more honest edit than relaxing a number.
- **`BAR_HEIGHT_PX` is deliberately not the bound.** It is already pinned in its own expectation by
  `expectStickyBar` clause 2; reusing it here would let the padding clause stay green off the back of
  a bar that grew.
- **`NaN` is asserted explicitly rather than left to the comparison.** `parseFloat("normal") >= 64` is
  false — safe in *this* operand order and silently green in the other, which is one refactor away.
- **The 320px call is part of the assertion.** Without it, "the padding is short in the band" cannot be
  told apart from "the helper is pointed at the wrong element at every width" — a red that looks like a
  finding and is a typo. It passed; that is what licenses reading the 640px red as a finding.
