<objective>
Make the three lapse-driven reclaim paths close the checkout session they orphan, at the moment they
orphan it.

13.1-04 shipped the retire policy and the sweep that applies it to every lapsed hold. That is the
guarantee, and it already makes D-113 true. This plan closes the gap between "the slot was freed" and "the
next tick" — the window in which the slot can be re-sold while the previous booker's session is still
payable.

Purpose: promptness, not correctness. Output: three call sites, all post-commit, none of which can be
broken by PayMongo being down.

⚠ **THIS PLAN IS AN ACCELERANT AND MUST STAY ONE.** If it were reverted tomorrow, a lapsed hold's session
is still retired within `RETIRE_INTERVAL_MINUTES` by `checkout-retire-sweep`. Nothing here may be made
load-bearing for D-113, and no code may be moved OUT of 13.1-04 into this plan.

**No new cron.** These are three existing code paths gaining a post-commit call; the cron inventory and
`src/app/api/inngest/route.ts` are untouched, so the residue discipline (04 at minute 4 step 5, plan 02's
reconcile at residue 2, the four hourly crons and the digest at residue 0) needs no re-derivation.
</objective>

## The three paths this plan closes

The full search and the out-of-scope list live in `13.1-04-PLAN.md`'s `<enumeration_finding>`. The three
lapse-driven paths it names, and which this plan wires:

1. **`src/lib/availability/units.ts:485-491`** — the exclusive in-tx stale-hold reclaim (D-48b) inside
   `createPendingHold`. Flips overlapping `pending|requested|approved` rows past `expires_at` to

2. **`src/lib/availability/units.ts:918-926`** — the SECOND reclaim, inside `createOpenCapacityHold`: open

3. **`src/inngest/functions/request-expiry.ts:96-124`** — `expireOne`, two branches. The
   `approved → cancelled` payment-window release is the sharpest of all three, because an `approved` row is
   BY DEFINITION one whose booker went to checkout: `src/app/actions/booking.ts:840` claims the session
   under `status IN ('pending','approved')`. Releasing it leaves a live payable session on a released slot.
   The `requested → declined` SLA branch is wired through the same call for uniformity and is a provable
   no-op: a `requested` row can never carry a `checkout_session_id`, by that same status scope. It is wired
   anyway so that the day request-to-book grows a pre-approval payment, the path is already closed.

<the_constraint_that_shapes_every_call_site>
## Outside the transaction. Not near it, not "usually", outside.

`createPendingHold`'s reclaim carries its own verdict, already in the file at line 482:

> *"Fire NO email in-tx — a Resend call inside this SAVEPOINT/rollback/retry tx is unsafe; the cron is the
> SOLE booker-email authority."*

A PayMongo call is bound by the same reasoning and by one more: the reclaim sits inside a transaction that
the outer `for (txAttempt)` loop can re-run wholesale on a 40P01, and inside a per-unit SAVEPOINT loop that
rolls back on 23P01. An in-tx provider call would fire once per attempt, against a session that the
rollback may have un-retired. And `createOpenCapacityHold`'s reclaim sits under an advisory lock whose own
comment reads *"NO EXTERNAL I/O MAY OCCUR BETWEEN THIS LINE AND COMMIT — no fetch, no email, no job emit."*

So the shape at both sites is the same three steps:

1. Add `RETURNING id, checkout_session_id, status` to the reclaim UPDATE. Nothing else in the statement
   changes.
2. Declare a mutable list OUTSIDE the `db.transaction` callback and **ASSIGN** (never push) the mapped rows
   to it inside. Assignment is the point: the whole transaction can be re-run, and a rolled-back attempt
   retired nothing, so a later attempt must REPLACE the previous attempt's list rather than accumulate it.
3. Retire AFTER the `db.transaction` promise resolves — which is after COMMIT — and then return.

**And the dependency that makes step 3 safe must be written at both call sites:** the retire is inside the
existing `try`, which is only acceptable because `retireCheckoutsForBookings` cannot throw (13.1-04 Task 1,
asserted three ways). If it could, a PayMongo outage would reach `mapBookingError`, fall through its
unknown-error re-throw, and make a booker's hold placement fail because a third party was down. State that
dependency explicitly so nobody later "hardens" the policy by making it throw.
</the_constraint_that_shapes_every_call_site>
