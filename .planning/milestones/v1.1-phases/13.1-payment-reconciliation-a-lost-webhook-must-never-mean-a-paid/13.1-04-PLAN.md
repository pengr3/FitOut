<objective>
Close the hole at its source: when a hold lapses, retire the checkout session with it.

Today FitOut frees the slot in its own database while the booker's checkout page, open tab or unscanned QR
still takes their money for a booking that no longer exists. This plan makes the ability to pay die with
the hold — the PM's instruction verbatim: *"booker shall not be able to do late payments — when the hold
expires, their ability to pay shall also expire, so we wouldn't handle money that isn't ours."*

Purpose: plans 01-03 reconcile the aftermath of money we should never have taken. This removes the case.
Output: one retire policy that cannot throw, the scheduled sweep that applies it to every lapsed hold, and
a proof that PayMongo itself reports the session non-payable afterwards.

⚠ **THIS PLAN IS THE GUARANTEE AND MUST STAND ALONE.** 13.1-05 wires the same policy into the three
in-transaction reclaim paths so a lapsed session dies in seconds instead of minutes. If 13.1-05 were never
written, D-113 is still true. Nothing here may be built assuming it lands.
</objective>

**NOT LAPSE-DRIVEN — found, inspected, correctly out of scope:** `booking.ts:651`/`:911` already call

`webhook/route.ts:194`/`:367` cancel BECAUSE a payment happened.

**⚠ AND THE FINDING THAT MADE THIS PLAN THE GUARANTEE RATHER THAN THE WIRING: the three paths above do not
cover the common case.**

A lapsed `pending` instant-book hold is flipped by **nothing** unless another booker happens to place an
overlapping hold. `request-expiry.ts` only selects `requested` and `approved`; no cron sweeps lapsed
`pending`. The lazy read predicates free the slot for display, but the row sits `pending` forever and **its
checkout session stays payable forever.** A plan that wired only the three enumerated paths would satisfy
the letter of the enumeration and leave the dominant case wide open — the exact "reads as closed" failure
the brief warns about, one level up.

That is why the sweep is here and why it is the guarantee.

<design_rules>
## Probe first, expire second — and never expire a paid session

`expireCheckoutSession` (`paymongo.ts:282`) already tolerates the repeat-expire 400 by re-probing and
accepting a provider-confirmed `expired` (its inner catch, `paymongo.ts:299-307`). It deliberately THROWS
on a `paid` session, because "money was CAPTURED on a session we were retiring" belongs in the caller's
`needs_attention` audit. Both behaviours are preserved untouched — **this plan does not edit `paymongo.ts`.**

The policy probes BEFORE it POSTs, for four reasons, all load-bearing:

1. **Evidence preservation in its strongest form.** A session the provider reports `paid` is never expired
   at all. Evidence cannot be destroyed by a call that is never made.
2. **`already-retired` is the steady state.** After the first retire the session reads `expired`, so every
   later visit costs ONE GET and zero POSTs — instead of a POST(400) plus a recovery GET each time.
3. **`probeCheckoutSession` cannot throw** (`checkout-probe.ts:69`, resolves `null` on anything), so the
   classification is total and the module's never-throw contract is structural, not defensive.
4. The residual TOCTOU — probe says `active`, the booker pays, the expire lands on a now-`paid` session —
   is exactly the case `expireCheckoutSession` throws on, producing a `needs_attention` row carrying
   `probedStatus: "active"`. The window is observable, not silent.

## ⚠ THE TRAP: DO NOT FLIP THE LAPSED ROW TO `cancelled`

This is the single most likely thing for a future reader to "tidy up", and it would silently destroy the
phase it belongs to. **This plan writes no `booking` row. Not one.** No status flip, no
`checkout_session_id` nulling, no marker column.

A lapsed `pending` hold is left `pending` ON PURPOSE. `pending` is precisely and only the status plan 02's
`queryUnconfirmedPaid` selects. Cancelling it would remove it from that selection, so a booker who paid at
the last second — the exact person this whole phase exists for — would never be reconciled, never be
confirmed, and never be told. Retiring the SESSION and retiring the BOOKING are two different facts and
only the first is D-113's. This rule must appear in the header of BOTH files this plan creates.

## What else this plan deliberately does not do

- **No copy.** `readPaymentState` (`checkout-probe.ts:118`) ALREADY names the state this creates: `pending`
  + session `expired` reads `"hold-expired"`, and `hold-expired-state.tsx` owns that landing. The visible
  consequence — a lapsed hold stops offering "finish paying in place" (the D-70 `not-completed` branch,
  `bookings/[id]/page.tsx:719`) — is exactly what D-113 asks for, with zero new strings and zero edits to
  that page (which 13.1-03 owns).
- **No migration (D-112).** The only thing a marker column would buy is skipping a GET on a row already
  retired; the lookback window buys the same bound for free.
</design_rules>
