# Phase 18.1: Close Phase 18 — the verification path FitOut is legally required to have - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-09-02
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

This phase builds **the thing that submits** to the console Phase 18 shipped.

Phase 18 delivered an ops console that *decides* hosts and listings, a provider-agnostic
verification port, a sell-gate with verification as a term, and a grandfather migration. It never
shipped a code path that creates a `host_verification` row from ordinary product use — so the ops
host queue can never fill, `approveHost()` flips zero rows and returns `STALE`, and no host created
after `drizzle/0026` can ever sell. That is a live production defect, not a missing feature.

18.1 closes it and discharges what RA 11967 § 21(b) obliges FitOut to hold before a listing goes up:

1. A host-facing **verification submission** that creates a real `host_verification` row.
2. **Didit** wired behind the existing port; the **manual** provider retained as the ops override.
3. § 21(b)(3) **contact facts** — a phone number and a valid email — required at submission.
4. The **listing-creation gate**, server-side, with a refusal that names the state and the way out.
5. **Ops contact reveal** on the queue, audited per reveal.
6. The three carried PM decisions: **D-236** (ops-cancel refunds the full charge), **D-231**
   (title + description are material fields), **F11** (a suspended host's earnings names the
   frozen session).

**Not in this phase:** the `/ops` staff-management surface and its invite flow (PM-A/PM-B → v1.2),
DTI E-Commerce Bureau registration (a company action), § 21(f) redress — booker-side reporting
(999.4) and host appeals (999.6).

## Implementation Decisions

### Carried in — settled before this discussion, recorded now as D-numbers

The ROADMAP and the two source todos each say *"record as D-numbers at discuss time."* Done here.
These are **ANSWERED. Do not re-ask, do not re-litigate.**

- **D-255 (PM-C)** — The verification gate sits at **listing creation**, not at publish and not at
  hosting entry. `canHost` still unlocks `/host` — dashboard, payouts onboarding, and the
  verification submission itself. A booker may still "Start hosting" and see what hosting involves.
  The **wizard refuses** for `unverified | pending | rejected | suspended`.
- **D-256 (PM-D)** — The submission path is built. The no-documents storage contract is
  **unchanged**: `host_verification` holds `{ result, vendorRef, checkedAt, provider }` and has no
  column that could hold a document, an ID number or an image.
- **D-257 (PM-E)** — Host contact in `/ops` is **reveal-on-click with an audit row per reveal**,
  never always-visible. The audit row carries **ids and enum-shaped values only** — never the
  email, never the phone, never the address (D-72; `src/lib/ops/grant.ts`'s header states the rule).
- **D-258 (PM-F)** — The vendor is **Didit**. PayMongo Linked Accounts rejected: its activation *is*
  its payouts gate, so it re-couples identity to payouts and undoes D-225 — and it is sales-gated,
  probed negative twice two months apart with its own docs page 404ing on both hosts.
- **D-259 (PM-G)** — The **manual provider stays registered** behind the port as the ops override:
  a vendor outage, an edge-case document, an appeal. Those rows carry `provider = 'manual'` plus an
  authenticated `actorId`, so the trail shows which decisions bypassed the machine. D-215 stands —
  one staff role, no tiers, no permission table.
- **D-260 (F11)** — A suspended host's `/host/earnings` **names the frozen session**. It does
  **not** name what unfreezes it: 18-13's deliberate refusal to promise a review with no SLA behind
  it stands.
- **D-236** (existing number) — `OPS_CANCEL_REFUNDS_SERVICE_FEE` flips to `true`.
- **D-231** (existing number) — `title` and `description` join the material-field set; photo
  reorder stays excluded.

### Didit's decision authority — what the vendor decides and what ops decides

- **D-261 — A Didit PASS auto-approves the host.** The port writes `approved` with
  `provider = 'didit'`, `vendorRef` and `checkedAt`. No operator confirms a pass. This is what
  paying a vendor buys, and it avoids re-imposing the PM-3 bottleneck where supply visibility is
  throttled to ops throughput.
- **D-262 — A Didit FAIL auto-rejects the host.** No operator confirms a fail either.
  ⚠ **PM decided against the SWE recommendation, with the consequence stated at decision time and
  accepted** — see § Accepted consequences below. Implement as decided; do not quietly soften it
  into "queue the fails for a human."
- **D-263 — There is no host-facing route to a human.** The manual provider is **ops-initiated
  only**. No control anywhere in the product implies a person will look at your case. This holds
  18-13's line — FitOut does not promise a review it has no SLA behind — and keeps host appeals
  cleanly in backlog 999.6.

### Re-submission — the only remedy left in the product after D-262

- **D-264 — A rejected host may retry, rate-limited.** Retry is the only thing standing between a
  vendor misread and a permanently unsellable real host.
  - **The limit is a 24-hour cooldown derived from `host_verification.updated_at`, NOT an attempt
    counter.** This is forced, not chosen: case 1 of `tests/ops/verification-schema.test.ts`
    asserts the table's column set by **set equality**, so a counter column reddens it. There is no
    lifetime cap because a lifetime cap cannot be expressed without one.
  - The existing in-memory limiter (`src/lib/rate-limit.ts`, the `activateHosting` precedent at
    `src/app/actions/capability.ts:64`, 5/60s keyed on user id) stays as the **burst guard only**.
    It is process-local and must never be described as the durable cap.
  - D-249's no-line-jumping rule is the precedent: a resubmission enters the queue at
    **resubmission time**, never at the original time. `host_verification_queue_idx` orders on
    `created_at ASC`.
- **D-265 — The auto-rejection `reason` stores Didit's own message.** There is no operator behind
  an auto-reject, so the host-readable sentence comes from the vendor.
  - Stored **bounded to the same 280-char ceiling** operator sentences carry, and rendered through
    the **same escaped path** (T-18-1301 — the operator's sentence already crosses a trust boundary
    on the host surface; a vendor's does too).
  - A **canned fallback sentence** when Didit sends nothing, or nothing usable. A host told "no"
    with an empty `reason` is the copy-about-a-check-that-never-ran defect one level up.
- **D-266 — A suspended host cannot re-submit.** The submission action refuses `suspended`
  outright. A suspension is a named staff member's deliberate act (ENF-01/D-233); letting the
  machine auto-approve a suspended host back to sellable would let the vendor silently reverse a
  human's enforcement decision. Only ops lifts it.

### RA 11967 § 21(b) — what FitOut collects, and where it lives

- **D-267 — The listing's address IS FitOut's § 21(b) geographic-address record.** No host address
  column, no new table, no migration.
  - The listing already carries a structured address — `addressLine1`, `addressLine2`, `city`,
    `postalCode`, and a PostGIS `location` point (D-10, nullable in draft, **required at publish**).
  - "Prior to listing" is satisfied because a listing cannot sell until ops has approved it
    (LVER-01) and the address is on the row the operator reviews.
  - **Do NOT add an address column to `host_verification`** — set equality on the allow-list makes
    that a decision to be argued, not merged.
- **D-268 — Phone is required at verification submission, self-declared, not verified.** Consistent
  with the address ruling: the statute says *collect*, not verify. Phone stays **optional on the
  profile** for bookers; a host must supply one to request verification. FitOut has no SMS provider
  (Resend is email-only), and adding one would put a new credential and a new failure mode on the
  critical path to selling.
- **D-269 — A confirmed email is required at verification submission.** Refuse until
  `user.emailVerified` is true, reusing the **shipped** resend affordance at
  `src/app/(host)/host/listings/[id]/edit/wizard.tsx:682-782` rather than inventing a new one.
  - This is **not** a new mechanism and **not** a reversal of D-07: `src/lib/auth.ts:67` keeps
    `requireEmailVerification: false` and `autoSignIn: true`, so sign-in stays unblocked globally.
    The gate is local to this action, exactly as the wizard's already is.
  - It matters more here than at publish: with D-262's auto-reject there is no operator to notice
    that a rejection notice bounced.

### SWE rulings — decided, not asked (PM may overrule)

- **D-270 — Existing drafts stay editable; only `createDraftListing` is gated.** The refusal lands
  in `createDraftListing` (`src/app/actions/listing.ts:93`). Gating `saveListingStep` as well would
  strand work hosts already did. This matches the source todo's own suggestion.
- **D-271 — The contact reveal goes on BOTH queue rows**, host and listing — ops questions are
  usually about a specific listing, and the contact needs to be where the subject of the question
  is. `mailto:` for email, **plain text** for phone (`tel:` is meaningless on a desktop console),
  explicit **"Not provided"** when a field is empty (the `addressOf()` precedent — an empty `<dd>`
  reads as a rendering bug).
  - ⚠ `ops-queue-row.tsx` is asserted **TERMINAL** — zero anchors, zero link-role elements — by its
    own test. A `mailto:` anchor needs an **explicit, documented exemption** in that assertion, not
    a deletion of it. Check `tests/` before adding the anchor.
  - The reveal must be a **server action under `requireStaff()` that returns the values and writes
    the audit row in the same call.** Do NOT ship the values in the initial RSC payload behind a
    CSS/`hidden` toggle — that is a reveal with no audit, and the payload is readable.

## Specific Ideas

- **The PM's framing on the address:** *the address box in the listing is the space's address —
  that is out of KYC's scope; the address is a listing thing, not a host thing.* Correct on both
  counts. § 21(b) is a **merchant-record** duty, not an identity one, and the ruling (D-267) is
  that the listing's own address discharges it. **Didit checks a person; the address describes a
  place, and the two are not the same record.**
- **D-269 was reframed mid-discussion by a factual correction.** The claim that "nothing in the
  product has ever enforced `emailVerified`" was **wrong** — the listing wizard already gates on it
  and already ships a resend. That is why D-269 is a reuse rather than a new mechanism.

## Accepted consequences — write-ups, not open questions

Recorded here so nobody rediscovers them as defects and "fixes" a decision.

1. **D-262 makes the ops host queue vendor-driven.** With both verdicts automatic and no
   host-facing route to a human (D-263), a `host_verification` row is `pending` only for the window
   between submit and Didit's answer. Success Criterion 1 — *"the queue fills from ordinary product
   use, proven by driving it"* — stays drivable **only if Didit answers asynchronously**. The human
   half of the console becomes reachable mainly through the ops-initiated manual override.
2. **A Didit false negative is close to a dead end.** Didit does not name the Philippines in its
   coverage and PhilSys support is unprobed. D-264's rate-limited retry is the **only** remedy a
   host can reach; appeals are 999.6.
3. **D-267's four gaps, accepted at ruling time.** A verified host who never publishes has **no
   address on file**. A host with three listings has three addresses and **no single merchant
   address**. Deleting the listing **takes the § 21(b) record with it**. And counsel gains a fourth
   question — *does a listing address discharge § 21(b) at all?* — alongside the two already open
   on § 21(c) and the subpoena clause.
4. **D-231's cost:** a material edit flips `review_state` to `pending` and `deriveBookable` requires
   `approved|grandfathered`, so **a typo fix in a description takes the listing off the market**
   until ops re-approves. Chosen over a "material but still sellable" variant that would need a
   state the sell-gate does not have.
5. **D-236 is NOT a one-line change**, despite three phase documents saying so. Production is one
   line; the test half is real work — case 1 asserts the constant is literally `false` and fails by
   design carrying its own "swap cases 1 and 2" message; `withFlippedConstant` hard-codes its mock
   to `true` and must become `false`; case 3's two expectation sets swap; and the docblock above the
   constant argues at length for the value being replaced. **Do not delete the mock helper** —
   exercising both branches is what makes the one-line-flip claim measured rather than asserted.
