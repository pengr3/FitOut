<objective>
Close the two reachable sources of new orphans, and do it without opening a destructive IDOR in the
phase that exists to prevent one.

Purpose: D-187 and D-188. Measured, not assumed: `destroyListingPhoto` has exactly ONE caller today —
`removePhoto` (`listing-photo.ts:293`) — and nothing else in the codebase ever deletes a Cloudinary
asset. So a listing holding 18 photos where the host selects 20 produces **18 unreachable,
permanently-billed assets from one click**: all 20 upload, 2 persist, 18 are refused at the cap,
`photo-uploader.tsx:113-121` toasts and returns, and nothing destroys anything. And
`softDeleteListing` sets `deletedAt` and destroys nothing, so a deleted listing's photos are billed
forever.

Output: two new CALLERS of an existing helper, and the four tests that make the constraint on one of
them impossible to lose in a refactor.

**⚠⚠ THE CONSTRAINT THAT MAKES THIS SAFE, AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL.** The destroy may run ONLY on a
refusal that happens **after `isOwnCloudinaryAsset` has PASSED**. It must NEVER run when provenance
REJECTED, and never on the session, ownership, empty-field or missing-cloud-name refusals that precede
it — because in all of those cases **we have no proof the `publicId` names an asset that is ours to
delete**. A destroy on the rejection path would hand any caller an arbitrary-delete primitive against
our own Cloudinary account: another host's cover photo, or `fitout/avatars/<victim-userId>`. That is
precisely the class of attack `src/lib/listing/cloudinary-provenance.ts` was written to stop. **The
cleanup would introduce a destructive IDOR inside the phase that exists to prevent it.** The shipped
order already makes the safe placement natural — provenance at `:130`, the cap at `:140` — and this
plan pins it with a behavioural test AND a source-ordering test.

**Why the ordering test is not redundant with the behavioural one.** A behavioural test is satisfied
by a destroy that merely happens to be skipped on the paths it exercises. The ordering assertion is
what stops the call MIGRATING UPWARD during a later refactor — someone "simplifying" the guards into
one block, or hoisting the cleanup into a shared helper at the top of the function. RESEARCH § I-6
states the assertion; PATTERNS states the hazard it must dodge, which is that a naive whole-file
substring check is falsely RED on a documented file.

**What this plan does NOT do.**

- **No sweep, no reaper, no Cloudinary Admin API diff.** D-187 declines it and D-190 is why that is
  proportionate rather than a compromise: production holds zero real host uploads, everything
  currently orphaned is a dev/test artefact (measured: 3 true orphans on the whole account, 0.15 MiB),
  and there is nothing worth the risk of an irreversible diff-driven delete. The home a sweep WOULD
  use is `src/inngest/functions/` and it stays empty of one.
- **No new helper.** `destroyListingPhoto` already exists and already sets CDN invalidation. Both
  sites are new CALLERS. Adding a third *helper* would break the occurrence count
  `src/lib/cloudinary.ts:125-128` records plan 16-12 as taking; adding callers elsewhere will not.
- **No schema change.** The `listing_photo` rows of a soft-deleted listing SURVIVE and their urls
  become 404s. Harmless — a soft-deleted listing is excluded from every read — but stated rather than
  fixed. GATE-06: `drizzle/` stays at `0025`.
- **No client-side destroy.** `addPhoto` in the uploader is unchanged; the client holds no credential.

**One planner call recorded rather than left implicit (RESEARCH Open Question 5).** The existing
`removePhoto` destroy at `:290-296` catches throws only, so the `{ result: "not found" }` RESOLVE
slips through silently. It is a log line, not a behaviour change, and D-187 does not ask for it — but
this plan is already editing that file and shipping two new sites on the stronger shape, and leaving
one of three call sites on the weaker one would guarantee the next reader copies the wrong one.
**Include it.** Use `console.warn` for the non-ok result at all three sites and keep `console.error`
for a thrown failure, so the two shapes are distinguishable in a log.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| client-supplied `{ publicId, url }` → `persistPhoto` | untrusted; `isOwnCloudinaryAsset` is the ONLY thing that turns it into "an asset of ours" |
| our server → Cloudinary's destroy endpoint | a credentialed, irreversible delete against our own account |
| `softDeleteListing` → the listing's photo rows | ownership is proven by `assertOwnership` before either the write or the destroy |
