================================================================================
THE CALL: NOT SMALL, NOT LOCALISED. ROWS 9-12 ARE NOT MINTED BY THIS PLAN.
================================================================================

Counted from the measurements above, the fix is THREE changes in THREE layers plus
a test, and one of the three is production source with a user-visible consequence:

  1. scripts/seed-baseline-fixtures.ts  - pin the host's created_at to a literal.
     Test-only, one line. Small on its own.
  2. src/lib/design/visual-baselines.ts - add `&today=` to the two failing URLs.
     Test-only, two string literals. Small on its own.
  3. src/components/availability/availability-calendar.tsx - pass `today={todayStart}`
     to <Calendar>. THIS IS PRODUCTION SOURCE AND IT IS A PRODUCT CHANGE, not a
     fixture change: today it is react-day-picker's dateLib.today() (the rendering
     HOST's day) and afterwards it is venue-local today. Those two disagree by a day
     for any venue in a timezone that differs from the server's. Whether the ring
     should be venue-local is a product question with a right answer that a fixture
     plan is not the place to decide - and note the answer is probably YES, which is
     precisely why it deserves to be decided rather than smuggled in under a
     baseline regeneration.
  4. A clock-independence test. This repository's standard for exactly this claim is
     tests/design/calendar-plate-month.test.tsx, which proves it THREE ways -
     behaviourally under two system clocks, structurally by census, and by a real
     renderToString -> hydrateRoot across a month boundary carrying a control
     required to FAIL. Anything weaker here is a green a decoy can counterfeit,
     which is the defect this whole phase exists to remove (see 19.1-06).

AND THE VERIFICATION ASYMMETRY, WHICH IS THE DECIDING ONE. The visual project is not
even constructed off Linux (D-29), so the effect of the fix on the rendered calendar
CANNOT BE OBSERVED BEFORE MINTING. Doing 1-4 and dispatching would put four reference
images into the repository whose stability is an assumption. If the ring fix is wrong
in any detail, the reference silently re-encodes "today is 5 September 2026" and the
rows are red again tomorrow - the exact unstable reference the PM forbade minting.

Under D-02 as the PM carried it forward, the phase therefore grows by a plan rather
than the diff being blessed. Rows 9-12 stay red, with the reason written down.

WHAT THAT PLAN HAS TO DO, so it does not have to re-derive any of the above:

  a. Decide, as a product question, whether the availability calendar's today ring
     should follow venue-local today (the value startMonth/endMonth/disabled already
     use) rather than the rendering host's clock. If yes, pass `today={todayStart}`.
     In production devTodayOverride returns null unconditionally, so todayLocal is
     wall-clock today and the change is byte-equivalent on every shipped request for
     a venue in the server's timezone; it differs only where the two clocks already
     disagree, which is the bug.
  b. Complete the 17-D26 seam it finishes: devTodayOverride was built to stop these
     four baselines expiring at the day-rollover, and it does not reach the one
     element that still expires. Say so at the site, or the next reader concludes the
     seam already covers it - as this plan initially did.
  c. Pin scripts/seed-baseline-fixtures.ts's host created_at to a fixed literal. The
     file's own header already forbids what line 193 does: "anything that varies
     between dispatches shows up as a pixel diff and trains people to re-mint the
     reference instead of reading the diff." now() is such a value. Check the other
     now() calls in seedUsers() at the same time.
  d. Add `&today=<the fixture's day>` to the listing-detail and collision-notice URLs
     in src/lib/design/visual-baselines.ts, and assert it in surfaces.spec.ts the way
     `date=` is already asserted there (the `?`-bearing-rows loop) - otherwise a
     future edit drops it and the rows silently go back to being clock-dependent.
  e. Carry the clock-independence test of point 4 above.
  f. THEN mint rows 9-12, and confirm stability by inspecting the minted PNG rather
     than by trusting the dispatch: the ring must land on the PINNED day, not on the
     day the dispatch ran. That check is available post-mint and costs one crop.
