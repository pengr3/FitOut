## § P1 — `/listings/[id]` under `next build && next start` (item 2, assumption `[A6]`)

### The question, and why it had to be asked in production

C1's argument, restated so this section stands alone: `[17-D1]` claims

implicit `<Suspense>` boundary and Next flushes the shell — committing `200` — before the page body
reaches `notFound()`. A **layout** renders in that shell, above the boundary, so awaiting there blocks
the flush and the status line is still settable. `assertPublicListing` is `cache()`d, so the layout's
lookup and the page's are one query.

### The honest limit, stated first

**The deletion itself cannot be watched red from the product side.** The file is unreachable in every
state (`§ P1` and `[17-D3]`: `(detail)/layout.tsx` raises `notFound()` from a **layout**, so the parent
segment's boundary wins, and `src/app/listings/[id]/` has none — it falls to the root). An unreachable
file's presence and its absence render **identically**, so no assertion that drives a browser can tell
them apart. There is no version of this drive that produces a red from a rendered document.

What CAN be watched is the **inventory machinery** — the four disk-walked gates that claim to know
which surfaces exist. That is a narrower claim than "the deletion is proven safe", and it is the one
made here: **the coupling between a deleted file and the instruments naming it is ENFORCED, not
remembered.** Three of the four reds below were driven; the fourth is the mirror of one of them and
is accounted for at the end without being manufactured.
