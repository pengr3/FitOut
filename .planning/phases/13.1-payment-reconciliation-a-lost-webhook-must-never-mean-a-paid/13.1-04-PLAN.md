<objective>
Close the hole at its source: when a hold lapses, retire the checkout session with it.

Today FitOut frees the slot in its own database while the booker's checkout page, open tab or unscanned QR
still takes their money for a booking that no longer exists. This plan makes the ability to pay die with
the hold — the PM's instruction verbatim: *"booker shall not be able to do late payments — when the hold
expires, their ability to pay shall also expire, so we wouldn't handle money that isn't ours."*

Purpose: plans 01-03 reconcile the aftermath of money we should never have taken. This removes the case.
Output: one retire policy that cannot throw, wired into every lapse-driven path, plus the sweep that covers
the lapse no path ever visits — proven against the live provider, not against a call count.
</objective>

<enumeration_finding>
## Every hold-retiring path, found by search — and the one that was not a path at all

The brief asks for the enumeration and warns that an incomplete one is the failure mode. Here is the full
search and its result. Searches run: `grep -rn "expires_at <= now()" src/`, `grep -rn "status = 'cancelled'|status = 'declined'" src/`, `grep -rn "expireCheckoutSession" src/`.

**LAPSE-DRIVEN — IN SCOPE, all three closed by this plan:**

1. **`src/lib/availability/units.ts:485-491`** — the exclusive in-tx stale-hold reclaim (D-48b) inside
   `createPendingHold`. Flips overlapping `pending|requested|approved` rows past `expires_at` to
   `cancelled|declined`. **`grep -c expireCheckoutSession src/lib/availability/units.ts` → 0.** This is the
   gap D-113 names.
2. **`src/lib/availability/units.ts:918-926`** — the SECOND reclaim, inside `createOpenCapacityHold`: open
   `pending` rows past `expires_at` → `cancelled`. Same defect, same file, **not named in D-113's evidence**
   and just as live: an open-capacity hold reaches checkout exactly like an exclusive one.
3. **`src/inngest/functions/request-expiry.ts:96-124`** — `expireOne`, two branches. The
   `approved → cancelled` payment-window release is the sharpest of all three, because an `approved` row is
   BY DEFINITION one whose booker went to checkout: `src/app/actions/booking.ts:840` claims the session
   under `status IN ('pending','approved')`. Releasing it leaves a live payable session on a released slot.
   The `requested → declined` SLA branch is handled by the same code path for uniformity, and is a provable
   no-op: a `requested` row can never carry a `checkout_session_id`, by that same status scope.

**NOT LAPSE-DRIVEN — found, inspected, correctly out of scope:**

- `src/app/actions/booking.ts:651` (`updateDeclaredPax` re-price) and `:911` (`confirmBooking`) — already
  call `expireCheckoutSession`. They are the precedent this plan follows, not work.
- `src/app/actions/cancel-booking.ts:654` and `:1074` — both flips are scoped `AND status = 'confirmed'`.
  A confirmed booking's session was already paid; there is nothing to retire and expiring it would be the
  evidence destruction constraint 4 forbids.
- `src/app/actions/host-requests.ts:319` (`declineRequest`) — flips `requested → declined`. A `requested`
  row provably has no session (same status scope as above). Nothing to retire.
- `src/app/api/paymongo/webhook/route.ts:194` (`handleGoneSlot`) and `:367` (refund branch) — these cancel
  BECAUSE a payment happened. Retiring there is meaningless and would be actively wrong.

**⚠ AND THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: the three paths above do not cover the common case.**

A lapsed `pending` instant-book hold is flipped by **nothing** unless another booker happens to place an
overlapping hold. `request-expiry.ts` only selects `requested` and `approved`; no cron sweeps lapsed
`pending`. The lazy read predicates free the slot for display purposes, but the row sits `pending` forever
and **its checkout session stays payable forever.** So a plan that wired only the three enumerated paths
would satisfy the letter of the enumeration and leave the dominant case wide open — the exact "reads as
closed" failure the brief warns about, one level up.

That is why this plan has a sweep, and why **the sweep is the guarantee and the three inline wirings are
the accelerant** — the same asymmetry plans 02 and 03 have. If Task 3 were reverted, a lapsed session is
still retired, within `RETIRE_INTERVAL_MINUTES`.
</enumeration_finding>

<design_rules>
## Probe first, expire second — and never expire a paid session

`expireCheckoutSession` (`paymongo.ts:282`) already tolerates the repeat-expire 400 by re-probing and
accepting a provider-confirmed `expired` (its inner catch, `paymongo.ts:299-307`). It deliberately THROWS
on a `paid` session, because "money was CAPTURED on a session we were retiring" belongs in the caller's
`needs_attention` audit. Both behaviours are preserved untouched — **this plan does not edit `paymongo.ts`.**

The retire policy probes BEFORE it POSTs, for four reasons, all load-bearing:

1. **Constraint 4 in its strongest form.** A session the provider reports `paid` is never expired at all.
   Evidence cannot be destroyed by a call that is never made.
2. **`already-retired` is the steady state.** After the first retire the session reads `expired`, so every
   later visit costs ONE GET and zero POSTs — instead of a POST(400) + recovery GET each time.
3. **`probeCheckoutSession` cannot throw** (`checkout-probe.ts:69`, resolves `null` on anything), so the
   classification is total and the module's never-throw contract is structural rather than defensive.
4. The residual TOCTOU — probe says `active`, the booker pays, the expire lands on a now-`paid` session —
   is exactly the case `expireCheckoutSession` throws on, which becomes a `needs_attention` row carrying
   `probedStatus: "active"`. The window is observable, not silent.

## What this plan deliberately does NOT do

- **It writes no `booking` row. Not one.** No status flip, no `checkout_session_id` nulling, no marker
  column. Retiring the session and retiring the booking are two different facts and only one is D-113's.
  Flipping a lapsed `pending` row to `cancelled` would remove it from plan 02's `queryUnconfirmedPaid`
  selection and actively break the phase's guarantee — the booker who paid at the last second would lose
  their reconciliation. This is the sharpest trap in the plan.
- **It adds no copy.** `readPaymentState` (`checkout-probe.ts:118`) ALREADY names the state this plan
  creates: `pending` + session `expired` reads `"hold-expired"`, and `hold-expired-state.tsx` already owns
  that landing. The visible consequence — a lapsed hold stops offering "finish paying in place" (the D-70
  `not-completed` branch, `bookings/[id]/page.tsx:719`) and shows the expired-hold landing instead — is
  precisely what D-113 asks for, and it needs zero new strings and zero edits to that page.
- **It adds no migration (D-112).** The only thing a marker column would buy is skipping a GET on a row
  already retired; the lookback window buys the same bound for free. GATE-06 and Phase 17 SC#4 stay intact.
</design_rules>
